What is included

T1 personal tax return

Preparation and filing of your T1 return — employment, self-employment, rental and investment income, and the slips and credits that apply to you.

Foreign property reporting (T1135)

If you hold specified foreign property, we help you report it correctly. A T1135 is generally required when the total cost of your foreign property is over $100,000 at any time in the year.

Non-resident property sales

Help for non-residents selling Canadian property — the forms, the withholding and the return that follows the sale.

Family tax planning

Planning questions for families: RRSPs, splitting income where the rules allow, and timing a sale or a move.

Owner-managers, too

If you also have a corporation, your T1 and your T2 are prepared together so the salary vs. dividend choice is consistent. See corporate tax (T2).

Who this is for

Individuals and families across Greater Vancouver — employees, the self-employed, landlords, newcomers and non-residents with Canadian tax to file — especially where there is foreign property or a property sale to report.

Most T1 returns are due April 30; self-employed individuals generally have until June 15 to file, though any balance owing is due April 30.

Common questions

Do I need to file a T1135?

If the total cost of your specified foreign property was over $100,000 at any time during the year, a T1135 is generally required — even if the property earned no income.

I am a non-resident selling a Canadian property. What do I need?

There are withholding and clearance steps before and after the sale, and a return to file. Talk to us before the sale closes if you can.

Can I get help in Chinese?

Yes. Service is in English and Chinese, and your return is explained in the language you prefer.
